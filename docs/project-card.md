# Project card

## The Second-Hour Tax

**Question.** Does random assignment of PISA 2025 *Learning in the Digital
World* (LDW) to the second rather than first testing hour change measured LDW
performance and students' measured comparative advantage across domains?

**Institutional mechanism.** PISA assigns students to rotated two-hour test
forms. Six forms create three mirrored pairs: science–LDW (books 5/6),
reading–LDW (9/10), and mathematics–LDW (11/12). Within each pair, one form
places LDW first and the other places it second.

**Population and unit.** 205,114 15-year-old students in 84 participating
systems, restricted to school × paired-domain strata containing both orders.
The unit of observation is the student; system-specific estimates are the unit
of cross-system synthesis.

**Treatment.** Assignment to an LDW-second form.

**Primary outcome.** Ten released LDW plausible values (`PV1CMPS`–`PV10CMPS`),
reported in PISA points and within-system standard deviations.

**Estimand.** The intention-to-treat effect of the randomized order/position
bundle on measured LDW proficiency among students in mixed-order school strata.

**Main specification.** Final-student-weighted regression with school ×
paired-domain fixed effects, estimated separately in every system. Inference
uses all 80 BRR-Fay replicate weights and combines all ten plausible values.
System estimates are summarized by fixed- and random-effects meta-analysis;
modified Hartung–Knapp and leave-one-system-out results audit the synthesis.

**Headline result.** LDW-second assignment lowers measured LDW performance by
8.32 points (SE 0.45; fixed-effect synthesis) or 9.00 points (SE 0.76;
random-effects synthesis), approximately 0.093 within-system SD. Seventy-eight
of 84 system estimates are negative.

**One-sentence contribution.** PISA's randomized form rotation shows that the
position of an autonomous digital-learning assessment changes not only its
reported score, but also measured cross-domain comparative advantage and the
engagement process through which that score is produced.

**Closest prior work.** OECD already reports first- versus second-hour effects
for PISA's conventional domains, and a large psychometric/economic literature
studies item-position effects and cognitive endurance. The defensible novelty
is therefore **LDW-specific**, not the generic discovery of test-order effects.

**Weakest indispensable assumption.** Conditional on the mirrored-domain pair,
the official random rotation was implemented without systematic within-school
sorting correlated with potential outcomes. Baseline balance is consistent with
this assumption, but cannot prove it.

**Most informative robustness boundary.** Requiring two students per order in
each stratum retains all systems and a similar effect. Higher thresholds retain
only 32 or 11 systems and change the target population; they cannot be read as
strictly nested estimates of one invariant international estimand.

**Interpretation boundary.** The treatment combines elapsed time, preceding
domain, warm-up, fatigue, priming, motivation, and any position-sensitive score
construction. It identifies a measurement effect under the PISA protocol—not a
permanent loss of human capital or a pure biological-fatigue parameter.

**Stage decision.** **Advance as a polished secondary writing sample and
measurement project.** It is empirically clean and distinctive in LDW, but its
broader test-position premise is established in prior work. For the user's
current portfolio, the Mozambique preschool RCT remains the safer default main
writing sample; PISA becomes competitive when an application values education
measurement, digital assessment, or large-scale survey methods.
