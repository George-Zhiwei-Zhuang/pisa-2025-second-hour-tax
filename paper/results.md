# Reproduced Empirical Results

## Main effect

Assigning LDW to the second testing hour reduces measured LDW performance by
8.318 PISA points (SE 0.445; 95% CI [−9.190, −7.446]) in the fixed-effect
meta-analysis. The random-effects estimate is 9.003 points (SE 0.758; 95% CI
[−10.489, −7.518]). In within-system units, the two estimates are −0.093 SD
(SE 0.005) and −0.093 SD (SE 0.007), respectively.

The sign is highly consistent across settings: 78 of 84 system-specific
coefficients are negative, and 39 are individually negative at the 5 percent
level. Heterogeneity is nevertheless nontrivial (`I² = 0.55` for the SD outcome),
which motivates reporting both meta-analytic summaries and the full system forest
plot.

## Cross-system robustness

The average effect is not an artifact of one synthesis rule. Modified
Hartung–Knapp inference gives −9.003 points (SE 0.795; 95% CI [−10.584,
−7.422]). The equal-system mean is −9.489 points (SE 0.844), the median system
effect is −8.539, the 10 percent trimmed mean is −8.819, and weighting by the
analysis sample size gives −9.839. A one-sided sign test for the 78 negative
system estimates has \(p<0.001\). Omitting one system at a time leaves the
random-effects estimate between −9.179 and −8.619 points.

The random-effects 95 percent prediction interval is [−19.877, +1.870] points
([−0.188, +0.003] SD). Thus the international mean is precisely negative and
not driven by any single system, but the model does not imply that every future
or individual participating system must have a negative effect.

## Support-threshold sensitivity

The strict main sample requires at least one student in each order within a
school × mirrored-pair stratum. Requiring at least two per order retains all 84
systems and 131,980 students; the random-effects estimate remains −8.303 points
(SE 0.814) or −0.087 SD (SE 0.008). More aggressive thresholds materially change
the population: five per order leaves 17,584 students in 32 systems and ten per
order leaves 10,898 students in 11 systems. Their point estimates are −13.425
(SE 3.874) and −3.829 (SE 2.536), respectively; the latter is not statistically
distinguishable from zero.

These high-threshold rows are not cleaner estimates of the same population. For
example, the ordinary main-sample estimate restricted to the 11 systems surviving
the ten-per-order rule is already only −6.604 points. Appendix Table A3 and Figure
A2 therefore present high thresholds as joint changes in local support and system
composition, not as a monotone robustness ladder.

## Measured comparative advantage

The mirrored pairs show the defining comparative pattern. In every pair, placing
LDW second lowers its measured score while placing the paired conventional domain
first raises that domain's measured score.

| Pair | LDW order effect (SD) | Conventional-domain order effect (SD) | LDW − conventional point contrast |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Science | −0.084 (0.008) | +0.078 (0.005) | −15.33 (0.91) |
| Reading | −0.114 (0.012) | +0.135 (0.011) | −23.01 (1.40) |
| Mathematics | −0.105 (0.012) | +0.063 (0.011) | −15.46 (1.14) |

Entries are random-effects meta-estimates with standard errors in parentheses.
The standardized components, rather than the cross-scale point difference, are
the preferred evidence that order changes students' measured relative strengths.

## Assignment balance

The treatment indicator is unrelated to all six audited predetermined variables.
Fixed-effect meta p-values range from 0.440 to 0.928. The differences are also
substantively tiny—for example, 0.0004 ESCS units and −0.0002 in the share female.
This pattern is consistent with the documented random rotation and offers no
evidence of differential sorting into the two form orders.

## Response and process summaries

Second-hour placement leaves the number of applicable LDW item records unchanged,
but it reduces normalized credit by 1.88 percentage points in the random-effects
synthesis. The reductions occur in learn (−2.22 pp), apply (−2.42 pp), and show
(−1.44 pp) phases. Responses coded not reached fall by 1.72 pp while responses
coded disengaged rise by 3.06 pp, so the former should not be read as improved
completion in isolation.

Students assigned to second-hour LDW record 16.55 percent less total time, 7.06
percent fewer actions, and 17.65 percent less mean first-action time across
applicable items. These results are
consistent with lower engagement under second-hour placement. Because the public
fields are item summaries rather than a complete event stream, they do not reveal
which tutorials, worked examples, or feedback paths students selected.

## Learner-type heterogeneity

Seven of 12 interaction estimates have BH q-values below 0.05. Higher
perseverance, curiosity, socioeconomic status, help seeking, and family support
are associated with a smaller second-hour penalty; greater digital distraction
is associated with a larger penalty. Test effort also attenuates the penalty but
is measured after assignment and is not a causal moderator.

The interaction magnitudes are modest—roughly 0.013 to 0.023 outcome SD per
moderator SD—and should remain secondary to the randomized average effect. The
self-regulation, AI-for-learning, feedback-seeking, ICT-feedback, and school-AI
interactions do not survive the multiplicity-adjusted threshold.

## Adaptive-science appendix

Adaptive science assignment changes science performance by 0.004 SD (SE 0.003;
p = 0.206). Its interaction with ESCS is −0.003 SD per ESCS SD (SE 0.004;
p = 0.462). The confidence intervals rule out large average or socioeconomic-gap
effects under this specification, but they do not establish exact equivalence.

## Reading guide

The empirical pattern supports a paper about the sensitivity of measured
achievement and cross-domain comparative advantage to randomized test order. It
does not establish a permanent skill loss or isolate a biological fatigue
mechanism. The appropriate headline is therefore: **where PISA places a domain
materially changes what it measures about the same target population.**
